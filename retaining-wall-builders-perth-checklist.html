Retaining Wall Builders Perth Checklist Explained

Bring order to the first conversation by treating the wall as a small civil job. Walk the boundary, driveway, slope and any upper terrace, then write down retained height, wall length, nearest structures and how machines or wheelbarrows could reach the line. The Perth retaining wall site decisions guide frames the same idea: appearance comes after height, soil, water and approval risk.

  • Measure the held-back ground, not only the neat face seen from the lawn.
  • Photograph the full slope, fence lines, access points and damp patches.
  • Note cracking, leaning, bulging or collapsed drainage behind an older wall.
  • Record whether another wall, paving, pool edge or structure sits above or below.

Approval triggers homeowners often miss

The published guidance points to a local government permit when retained ground is over half a metre. It also warns that height alone is not the whole test: approval can still be relevant where the work connects with other building activity, protects or affects neighbouring land, or interacts with a boundary wall or dividing fence. Tiered terraces deserve extra care because Wanneroo and Melville are named as councils that may treat combined tier height as the trigger.

For engineered walls, the source references AS 4678 and design by WA-registered building engineering practitioners. A quote should state whether engineering, drawings, permit lodgement and council submission are inside the price or excluded. A low figure that quietly leaves them out can become costly once the approval path is clarified.

Match the system to the block

Limestone block is discussed as a common Perth choice for sandy Swan Coastal Plain sites and local streetscapes. Ask whether the quote means natural or reconstituted limestone, what footing is assumed, and whether block size fits the retained height. Post and panel construction uses posts with precast panels slotted between them, giving a fast, consistent finish often seen along driveways and boundaries across the metro area.

Besser block is described for taller engineered structural work and rendered finishes. Timber sleeper walls suit smaller garden-scale retaining, while rock and boulder walls are linked with Perth Hills blocks and granite-outcropped sites. Do not treat these as swap-for-swap products: surcharge, drainage, height and access decide fit.

Drainage belongs in the written scope

The source material treats drainage as structural, not decorative. It names ag drain, gravel and geofabric behind every wall and links that detail to resisting failure in winter-saturated sandy ground. This is where similar-looking quotes can separate sharply, because excavation depth, backfill type, outlet position and removal of failed old drainage may be included by one builder and omitted by another.

  • Ask for the drainage method in plain writing.
  • Separate face material from footing, backfill and outlet detail.
  • For repairs, start with failure mode, footing condition, drainage and material state.
  • Compare patchable defects against walls needing partial demolition and rebuild.

Who this checklist suits

This angle is most useful for owners planning a new wall, replacing a failing one, or confused by one quote including engineering while another does not. It also helps where boundaries, driveways, tiered level changes or adjoining land make approval less obvious. It is less relevant to decorative edging that holds little soil, though drainage and material life still matter there. For a shorter framing of the same topic, see the parent guide on local retaining wall decisions in Perth.

  1. Walk and measure. Record retained height, length, boundaries, slope, access and visible distress before pricing.
  2. Test approval risk. Check the half-metre trigger, tier counting, boundary issues and links to other building work.
  3. Shortlist systems. Compare limestone block and post and panel against sleeper, besser or rock only where site facts support them.
  4. Demand comparable scope. Ask each quote to state drainage, footings, backfill, engineering, permits and exclusions in the same format.

Common questions

Is half a metre the only permit test? No. The guidance uses retained ground around that mark as a common trigger, but lower walls can still need review where they affect adjoining land, boundary structures or other building work.

Which wall type is best for Perth homes? There is no universal best. Limestone block and post and panel are the two main systems named locally, while sleeper, besser and rock suits narrower cases shaped by height, surcharge, drainage and site access.

What makes one quote cheaper than another? Often scope, not material alone. Excavation, backfill, ag drain, geofabric, engineering, permit drawings and council submission can be included or left out, changing the apparent price.
